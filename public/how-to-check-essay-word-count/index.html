How to check an essay word count

Most assignments set a word limit, not a page count. A reliable word count helps you stay inside that range before you submit. You can check this in your browser with Free Word Counter; the text stays on your device.

Steps

  1. Paste or type your draft. Open Free Word Counter and paste the essay body into the text box. If your brief excludes the title, bibliography, or footnotes, paste only the parts that count.
  2. Read the word and character totals. The tool updates the word count, character count, sentence count, and an estimated reading time as you type or paste.
  3. Trim or expand with the extras. If you are over the limit, use the companion text tools (for example extra-space removal) only when they do not change meaning. If you are under, add evidence or explanation rather than filler.
  4. Recheck the final file. After you format the essay in a word processor, paste the final text once more. Hidden comments or tracked changes in a desktop file can make two counters disagree.

What usually counts toward a limit

Follow the rule in your brief or style guide. Many schools count the main text only and exclude the title page, reference list, and appendices. If the instructions are silent, ask the teacher or editor rather than guessing.

Why two tools can disagree

Word processors and websites do not always split words the same way. Hyphenated terms, numbers, and citations are common sources of small differences. Use one method consistently, and allow a small margin under a hard maximum.

When you are ready, open Free Word Counter and paste your draft. The count runs in your browser.
