How to estimate speaking time for a speech

Whether you are preparing a wedding toast, a class presentation, a pitch or a video script, the question is the same: will it fit in the time I have? Counting words is the quickest way to get a first estimate before you rehearse.

Steps

  1. Paste only what you will say. Open Free Word Counter and paste the final script, without speaker notes, slide titles you will not read, or stage directions.
  2. Read the word count and the speaking time. The tool shows an estimated speaking time based on about 150 words per minute, and a silent reading time based on about 200 words per minute. Both are rounded up to the next minute.
  3. Measure your own pace. Start a timer and read the script aloud for exactly one minute, at the speed you will really use. Count the words you covered: that is your personal words-per-minute.
  4. Fit the script to your slot. Multiply your pace by the minutes you have. That is your word budget. Cut or add text until the word count is a little under it, then recount.

A worked example

You have 5 minutes and your script is 900 words. At 150 words per minute that is 6 minutes, one minute too long. Cutting to about 700 words leaves room for pauses, a slower start and audience reactions.

Why the estimate can be off

Speaking pace varies a lot between people, languages and situations. Nerves tend to make people speak faster; pauses for slides, demos, laughter or questions add time that a word count cannot see. Technical words, numbers and names also take longer to say. Use the estimate to plan, then rehearse with a timer at least once.

Leave a margin

For a strict time limit, aim for roughly 10 to 20 percent under your word budget. It is easier to slow down than to rush the ending.
